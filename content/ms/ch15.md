## The coffee cooling problem

You order coffee and a little cup of cold milk. You want to drink it in a few minutes, as hot as possible. Should you add the milk **now** or **just before drinking**? This chapter builds the cooling model; chapter 16 answers the question.

## Temperature and heat

**Heat** is energy; **temperature** measures how hot something is. Adding heat Q to a mass m raises its temperature by ΔT = Q / (m·c), where c is the **specific heat capacity** (for water, about 4.2 J/g/°C). Heat moves by **conduction** (through the cup), **convection** (air carrying it away) and **radiation**.

```predict
m, c = 300, 4.2          # grams, J/g/degC
Q = -m * c * 20          # heat lost when 300 g of water cools by 20 degC
print(Q)
print(Q / (m * c))
```

## Newton's law of cooling

All three mechanisms are complicated, so the book uses a simple empirical law: the rate of cooling is proportional to the **difference** between the object's temperature and its environment:

dT/dt = −r·(T − T_env)

r depends on the cup, the liquid and the air; we'll estimate it from a measurement.

```exercise

kind: write
---prompt
Write `update_func(state, t, system)`: read `T = state.T`, compute the change `-system.r * (T - system.T_env) * system.dt`, and return `State(T=T + change)`.
---starter
def update_func(state, t, system):
    pass
---solution
def update_func(state, t, system):
    T = state.T
    dT = -system.r * (T - system.T_env) * system.dt
    return State(T=T + dT)
---tests
s = update_func(State(T=90.0), 0, System(r=0.01, T_env=22, dt=1))
assert abs(s.T - (90 - 0.68)) < 1e-9, f"0.01 x (90 - 22) = 0.68 degrees lost in one minute; got {s.T}"
s = update_func(State(T=22.0), 0, System(r=0.5, T_env=22, dt=1))
assert s.T == 22.0, "At room temperature, nothing changes"
```

```quiz
A cup of coffee at 90 °C and one at 40 °C sit in a 22 °C room. Under Newton's law, which loses temperature faster right now, and by how much?
* The 90 °C cup, about 68/18 ≈ 3.8 times faster
- The 40 °C cup, because it's closer to the room
- Both the same
- Neither, until they touch something cold
> The rate is proportional to the temperature difference: 68 °C vs 18 °C. Hot things cool fast; the cooling slows as they approach room temperature.
```

```run
title: Coffee for 30 minutes
def update_func(state, t, system):
    T = state.T
    return State(T=T - system.r * (T - system.T_env) * system.dt)

def make_system(T_init, r, volume, t_end):
    return System(init=State(T=T_init), r=r, volume=volume, T_env=22, t_0=0, t_end=t_end, dt=1)

def run_simulation(system, update_func):
    frame = TimeFrame(columns=system.init.index)
    frame.row[system.t_0] = system.init
    for t in linrange(system.t_0, system.t_end, system.dt):
        frame.row[t + system.dt] = update_func(frame.row[t], t, system)
    return frame

coffee = make_system(T_init=90, r=0.01, volume=300, t_end=30)
results = run_simulation(coffee, update_func)
plot(results.T, label="coffee")
decorate(xlabel="Time (minutes)", ylabel="Temperature (C)")
print("after 30 minutes:", round(get_last_value(results.T), 1), "C")
```

The book's measurement: the coffee is **70 °C after 30 minutes**. With r = 0.01 the model says about 72 °C, so r must be a bit bigger. Chapter 16 finds it exactly with a root finder.

```recall
Q: State Newton's law of cooling and explain how it becomes an update function.
A: - dT/dt = −r(T − T_env): cooling rate proportional to the temperature difference with the surroundings.
- In discrete steps of length dt: T_next = T − r·(T − T_env)·dt.
- r is fitted to a measurement (e.g. 70 °C after 30 min); the time step must multiply the rate.
```
