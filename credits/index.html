The short version

  • Some tracks follow open textbooks, credited below with links to read them free.
  • Every lesson, world, simulation and exercise here was written for Tulip Labs.
  • We're not affiliated with, or endorsed by, the authors or publishers named here.

Open books

  • 3D worlds and Complexity in code follow Think Complexity, 2nd edition, by Allen B. Downey (Green Tea Press).
  • Modeling & Simulation follows Modeling and Simulation in Python by Allen B. Downey (Green Tea Press). Our modsim.py is a short rewrite that keeps the book's function names.
  • Structures follows Open Data Structures by Pat Morin.

All three are free to read: greenteapress.com and opendatastructures.org. They're published under open licences, and we follow their terms.

The models

The worlds and lessons are built around models first described by Thomas Schelling; Paul Erdős and Alfréd Rényi; Duncan Watts and Steven Strogatz; Albert-László Barabási and Réka Albert; Stephen Wolfram; John Conway; Per Bak, Chao Tang and Kurt Wiesenfeld; Joshua Epstein and Robert Axtell; Mitchel Resnick; Craig Reynolds; Stuart Kauffman; and Robert Axelrod.

Data

  • Facebook friendship network: the SNAP collection, Stanford University.
  • World population: rounded US Census Bureau estimates.
  • The glucose lessons use synthetic data generated from the minimal model, not patient records.

Software

Python in the browser runs on Pyodide, with NumPy, SciPy, pandas, NetworkX, Matplotlib and SymPy. 3D is drawn with Three.js. Thanks to everyone who builds and maintains them.

If we've left out a credit or got one wrong, open an issue on GitHub and we'll fix it quickly.
